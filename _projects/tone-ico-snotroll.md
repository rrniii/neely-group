Antarctica's escarpment lies between the coast and the high polar plateau. Its steep terrain, exposed rock, blue ice and snowfields influence how air moves, clouds form and snow accumulates. **TONe–ICO and SnøTroll** bring sustained observations to this challenging region to investigate how those processes affect the ice sheet.

## TONe–ICO: year-round observations at Troll

The **Integrated Cloud Observatory (ICO)** at Troll Station is part of the **Troll Observing Network (TONe)**, operated by the Norwegian Polar Institute in Dronning Maud Land. Ryan was part of the international team that installed the observatory in 2025, alongside colleagues from Norway and the United States.

The instrument suite combines scanning cloud radar, depolarisation lidar, a microwave radiometer and an automated radiosonde launcher. Together, these provide complementary measurements of clouds, precipitation and the surrounding atmosphere. Scanning radar extends the observations across the rugged landscape, detecting clouds and precipitation up to 15 kilometres away. Radiosondes measure temperature, humidity and wind through the atmosphere, providing the context in which clouds develop. [Read the NCAS field report](https://ncas.ac.uk/observatory-in-antarctica-enables-scientists-to-study-clouds-like-never-before/).

## SnøTroll: from terrain to snowfall

**SnøTroll** is a collaboration between researchers in the UK, Norway and the United States. Its **2026–2028 NERC award** is led at Leeds by Ryan, with Ian Brooks and Stephen Hudson as co-investigators and Heather Guy and Sarah Barr as researchers. It builds on ICO observations and infrastructure to study the movement of moisture and energy through the Antarctic escarpment. [View the active award and project team](https://gtr.ukri.org/projects?ref=UKRI4313).

The research connects three questions:

- How do the different surfaces and steep terrain shape local weather?
- How do those conditions affect clouds and snowfall?
- How well do weather and climate models represent the processes we observe?

The project brings additional ground-based and airborne observations together with model evaluation. It contributes to **Antarctica InSync** and works with the WMO's **Polar Coupled Analysis and Prediction for Services (PCAPS)** initiative. [Read the public SnøTroll project record](https://gtr.ukri.org/projects?ref=UKRI4313).
