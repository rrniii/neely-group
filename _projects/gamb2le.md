The **Greenland Automated Mass Balance and Boundary Layer Experiment (GAMB2LE)** connects instrument engineering with a central polar science question: how do atmospheric conditions control the energy and mass exchanged at the Greenland Ice Sheet's surface?

GAMB2LE is led by the National Centre for Atmospheric Science as part of ARIA's **Forecasting Tipping Points** programme. Ryan Neely, Heather Guy and Sarah Barr share project leadership, bringing together expertise in clouds, remote sensing, polar fieldwork and instrument development. [Meet the GAMB2LE team](https://www.gamb2le.co.uk/team).

## AURORA: an observatory built for remote science

The **Automated Unit for Remote Observations and Research of the Atmosphere (AURORA)** is the platform at the centre of GAMB2LE. Its design integrates surface remote sensing and in situ instruments with uncrewed aircraft and renewable power. The modular system is intended to support sustained measurements in locations that are difficult to reach and rarely observed. [Explore the AURORA design](https://www.gamb2le.co.uk/about/arctic-hardeded-mobile-observatory).

The aim is to bridge a gap between small autonomous weather stations and large research stations: collecting a more complete picture of the atmosphere while keeping the system portable and limiting the infrastructure it needs. Measurements of clouds and near-surface conditions will help researchers investigate the processes driving the ice sheet's surface energy and mass budgets. [Read the scientific rationale](https://www.gamb2le.co.uk/about/about-the-greenland-ice-sheet).

## What the instruments tell us

No single instrument captures the whole ice–atmosphere system. AURORA combines complementary observations:

- **Cloud radar** reveals cloud height, thickness and internal variability, helping us investigate cloud structure and the processes within it.
- **Microwave radiometry** provides information about atmospheric liquid water and the vertical profiles of temperature and humidity. These quantities help explain how the atmosphere affects the surface energy budget.
- **Radiation and weather sensors** measure conditions near the surface, connecting the atmosphere above with the environment at the observing site.

The science system coordinates the instruments and transfers their observations to storage and the project data viewer. A separate power system manages energy supply and battery charging. [Explore the instrument science](https://www.gamb2le.co.uk/latest-news/rpg-training) and [the observatory's construction](https://www.gamb2le.co.uk/latest-news/aurora-development).

## From observations to understanding change

Sustained measurements must capture both the processes at one location and how those processes change with time. GAMB2LE develops the observing capability needed to study these interactions and contribute evidence to the wider effort to understand Greenland's potential tipping points. [Read the project overview](https://www.gamb2le.co.uk/about).
